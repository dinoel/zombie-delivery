FIELD MANUAL

Being seen

A zombie only watches the arc in front of itself, and notice distance depends on what you are doing: the front ranges are roughly 150 while sprinting, 90 while walking, 45 while still, and 28 while crawling. Everything shrinks to about a third from behind. Hold C to crawl slowly with almost silent footsteps; a flashlight beam can still betray you instantly. The HUD meter shows the most suspicious zombie, while the filling arc shows exactly who is working you out. Come up from behind with the light off and press E for a silent kill.

The horde

The horde flanks, dodges the firing line, and surges after a retreating courier. Every type throws a projectile with its own colour and speed — and a zombie splattered by a neighbour's throw turns on the thrower instead of you. You have 5 seconds to look around after spawning; your first shot ends that grace period.

Tanks

Tanks roam in packs of three or four, far from the parcels: huge, half as fast as a walker, no tactics at all, and immune to silent kills. Traffic steers around a tank instead of flattening it — a car that hits one wrecks its own front and barely survives a second collision. Walking around a pack costs far less than fighting it.

The street

Cars run zombies down, but crashes crumple bodywork and smash windows and lights; a wrecked car smokes and becomes cover. A patrol car siren draws zombies, and the officers shoot back — their gunfire pulls the horde toward the car rather than toward you. Batteries and ammo are scattered through the yards, and most of the horde drops cash — a tank always does. Nothing sells anything yet, so the wallet just fills up over the shift.

Ground and weather

Pavement is faster and louder; rain muffles sound, while thunder breaks the trail. On a phone the first finger moves and the second fires.
